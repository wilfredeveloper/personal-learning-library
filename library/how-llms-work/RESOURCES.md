# How LLMs work: resources

## Knowledge

- [Repo: how-llms-work](https://github.com/w3cj/how-llms-work)
  The course's spine. Its README lists the 11 papers, in the order the concepts appear. Use for: every lesson's file:line anchors.
- [Book: _Speech and Language Processing_ (3rd ed. draft, Aug 2026), Jurafsky & Martin](https://web.stanford.edu/~jurafsky/slp3/)
  Free, authoritative textbook. Ch. 3 n-gram LMs & chain rule; ch. 2 tokens (BPE); ch. 5 embeddings; ch. 6 neural nets & backprop; ch. 7 transformers. Use for: primary reading in most lessons.
- [Video: "Let's build GPT: from scratch, in code, spelled out", Andrej Karpathy (2023)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kCc8FmEb1nY)
  Lecture 7 of Neural Networks: Zero to Hero (~2 h). Use for: Part IV, the transformer and generation loop in code.
- [Course: Neural Networks: Zero to Hero, Andrej Karpathy](https://github.com/karpathy/nn-zero-to-hero)
  Backprop by hand (micrograd) through GPT. Use for: lessons 2–3 and 6–9.
- [Spec: WHATWG HTML §9.2 Server-sent events](https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/server-sent-events.html)
  The normative event-stream format. Use for: the pipe (lesson 1).
- Papers cited by the repo (links in its README): Weizenbaum 1966; Rumelhart, Hinton & Williams 1986;
  Glorot & Bengio 2010; Mikolov et al. 2013a/b; Kingma & Ba 2014; Sennrich et al. 2016; Ba et al. 2016;
  Vaswani et al. 2017; Radford et al. 2018; Holtzman et al. 2019. Use for: the primary source of each lesson.

  Research-focused, moderated. Use for: paper discussions once you're reading primary sources.
- [r/learnmachinelearning](https://www.reddit.com/r/learnmachinelearning/)
  Beginner-friendly. Use for: "is my understanding of X right?" questions.

  Open research community that trains real LLMs. Use for: talking to people who build these.
